MiroFish: test your idea on a crowd

Drop in your idea as a short document. A swarm of AI agents talks it through on a simulated social network, and you get a report plus the chance to question every agent. Every group has its own instance and history. Log in with your group name, for example gruppe3, and the password from your card.

One run, start to report

About 10 minutes. Start a run early, then keep building while it works.

  1. Upload a seed

    One to three pages about your idea (PDF, Markdown or TXT). Write your question into Simulation requirement, for example: “How do students in Mannheim react to this offer?”

  2. Graph builds itself about 1 min

    MiroFish pulls people, organisations and relations out of your seed.

  3. Agents get profiles 1–2 min

    Each entity becomes an agent with its own background and opinions.

  4. Run the simulation about 2 min

    Runs are capped at 10 rounds to keep them quick. More rounds rarely add new arguments anyway.

  5. Generate the report 4–6 min

    Afterwards, open Interaction and ask single agents why they reacted the way they did.

  • Read the result as a scenario, not a forecast. The agents argue plausibly; they don't predict the future.
  • Reloading is safe. Your run keeps going on the server and the progress comes back.
  • For a finished run, open only Report and Interaction. Opening its Environment setup or Simulation step starts that step again.

Outlook: AI that works on AI

Two pieces of news from this week, and the big question behind them.

  • An AI edited another AI's weightsMIT researchers had OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra change the weights of a small open model directly, without retraining. Touching 0.016% of the weights lifted its math score from 7.9% to 48.1%, mostly by stopping it from talking in loops. It did not add new knowledge, and no self-improving cycle was shown. VentureBeat, 2 November 2026.
  • Anthropic expects recursive self-improvement to come closeIn its IPO filing, the company behind Claude writes that its estimates for future models suggest they “may imminently” reach its threshold for recursive self-improvement: AI that substantially speeds up building the next AI. No current model has crossed it, and Anthropic wants humans to stay in control. TechCrunch, 2 November 2026.
  • What is the singularity?The idea that AI improves itself in a loop, each generation building a better next one, until progress gets too fast to predict. The term goes back to the mathematician Vernor Vinge (1993); Ray Kurzweil puts it around 2045. Whether and when it comes is wide open, and researchers disagree sharply.

What it means for your idea: the tools change every few months. The problem you solve and the people you solve it for stay.

From value proposition to clickable prototype

No coding needed, just free accounts. Your goal: one user, one path, a shared link that works in the pitch.

  1. Build brief

    Your value proposition in one sentence: for whom, which problem, which benefit.

  2. One path

    Start, two or three steps, result. More than five screens won't fit into the time.

  3. Start prompt

    Fill in the template below and send it.

  4. Refine

    Click through, then exactly one wish per prompt.

  5. Demo

    Share the link, rehearse a 60-second demo, keep three screenshots as an emergency slide.

The demo in your pitch

  1. One user, one path

    Show how your target group solves its problem. Not every feature.

  2. Test it before

    Open the link on the pitch laptop before it's your turn.

  3. Emergency slide

    Three screenshots in your slides, in case the link or Wi-Fi hangs.

Pitch honestly: say what you built with AI. A prototype looks real but can't do anything yet. That belongs in one sentence of your demo.

Prompt templates

Replace the highlighted parts, then copy. The templates are in German, as on the cheat sheet; writing them in English works just as well.

Start prompt

Baue einen klickbaren Prototyp einer Web-App auf Deutsch. Zielgruppe: [wer genau]. Ihr Problem: [was sie heute nervt]. Unser Werteversprechen: [euer Satz aus dem Value Proposition Canvas]. Die Nutzerin geht diesen Weg: [Screen 1] → [Screen 2] → [Screen 3] → [Ergebnis]. Nutze realistische Beispieldaten, keine echten Daten. Kein Login, keine Datenbank. Einfaches, freundliches Design, gut lesbar auf dem Handy.

Change something

Ändere auf dem Screen [Name] nur [was]. Lass alles andere, wie es ist.

Add a screen

Füge nach [Screen] einen Screen hinzu, auf dem die Nutzerin [was tut].

Button doesn't work

Der Button [Beschriftung] reagiert nicht. Er soll zu [Screen] führen.

Sharpen the copy

Mach alle Texte kürzer und verständlicher für [Zielgruppe].

Sample data

Ersetze die Platzhalter durch 5 realistische Beispiele für [was].

Fix an error

Es erscheint dieser Fehler: [Fehlertext]. Behebe ihn, ohne das Design zu ändern.

What doesn't belong in an AI tool

  • Real names, addresses, photos or contact details of people
  • Confidential documents from partners or companies
  • Passwords and access data
  • Your own sensitive data, for example about health or finances

This applies to MiroFish seeds too: fictional or public material only.
